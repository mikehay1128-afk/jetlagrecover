Jet Lag Guide

Understanding jet lag

Everything below is general educational information — not medical advice.

01

How long does jet lag last?

There's no single number that applies to every traveler. As a general pattern, adjustment time tends to track with how many time zones you crossed and which direction you flew, plus factors like how well you slept before the trip, your itinerary once you land, and simple individual variation. Some travelers feel mostly settled within a couple of days; others take closer to a week for a long-haul, multi-zone trip. Treat any specific number — including the estimate this calculator gives you — as a general starting point rather than a fixed timeline.

02

Jet lag symptoms

Jet lag symptoms show up because your internal body clock and the local time at your destination are temporarily out of sync. Commonly reported jet lag symptoms include:

  • Trouble falling asleep or staying asleep at the destination's normal bedtime
  • Daytime fatigue or excessive sleepiness
  • Difficulty concentrating or a general sense of mental fog
  • Waking up much earlier or later than intended
  • Irritability or low mood
  • Mild digestive changes or general malaise

Symptoms are usually temporary and tend to ease as your schedule realigns with local time over the following days. This is general educational information, not a diagnostic checklist — if symptoms are severe or don't improve, consider speaking with a qualified healthcare professional.

03

What causes jet lag?

Jet lag happens when your internal body clock — the roughly 24-hour rhythm that governs when you feel sleepy and when you feel alert — is still running on your departure time zone after you've landed somewhere with a different local time. Your body clock doesn't reset the moment you step off the plane; it catches up gradually, which is why you might feel wide awake at 2 a.m. local time or foggy in the middle of the destination afternoon.

04

Eastward vs. westward jet lag

Direction of travel matters because of how the body clock naturally moves. Most people find it a little easier to stay up later and sleep in (which is what westward travel effectively asks of you) than to fall asleep and wake up earlier than usual (which is what eastward travel asks of you). This is a general tendency, not a rule — individual results vary, and the number of zones crossed still matters more than direction alone once you're crossing many time zones.

05

How to adjust your sleep schedule before traveling

When your schedule allows, gradually shifting your bedtime and wake time in the days before departure — a little each day, in the direction your destination requires — can give your body clock a head start. This calculator suggests a simple pre-travel shift based on your itinerary. It's a general strategy, and it isn't always practical depending on work, family, or travel schedules — skipping it doesn't mean you'll necessarily struggle more.

06

How to recover from jet lag

General, non-medical habits many travelers find helpful:

  • Try to align meals and sleep with the destination's local schedule as soon as practical.
  • Get some daylight exposure at times that support your target schedule, when possible.
  • Stay hydrated during and after the flight.
  • Keep your first day or two relatively light on demanding commitments if your itinerary allows.
  • Give your schedule a few consistent days rather than expecting an overnight fix.

If sleep difficulties are significant, persistent, or affecting your wellbeing, consider speaking with a qualified healthcare professional.

FAQ

Common questions

How does the Jet Lag Recovery Calculator work?+

It takes your departure and destination time zones, arrival date and time, and your normal sleep schedule, then estimates the number of time zones crossed and travel direction. From there it generates a general, personalized sleep schedule and recovery timeline. All calculations happen instantly in your browser.

How many time zones do I need to cross to experience jet lag?+

Many travelers notice little to no jet lag crossing one or two time zones. Effects tend to become more noticeable somewhere around three or more zones, though this varies quite a bit from person to person.

What are the most common jet lag symptoms?+

Commonly reported jet lag symptoms include trouble falling or staying asleep at the destination's normal bedtime, daytime fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, and waking up earlier or later than intended. Symptoms are usually temporary and tend to ease as your schedule realigns with local time.

Is eastward or westward travel harder to adjust to?+

As a general pattern, many people find eastward travel (which requires falling asleep and waking up earlier) somewhat more difficult than westward travel (which requires staying up and waking later). This is a tendency, not a rule, and the number of zones crossed matters too.

How long does jet lag usually last?+

It varies by individual and trip, but adjustment time generally scales with the number of time zones crossed and travel direction. This calculator gives a general estimated range based on your specific itinerary.

Can I adjust my sleep schedule before traveling?+

Yes, when your schedule allows it. Gradually shifting your bedtime and wake time toward your destination's schedule in the days before departure is a commonly suggested general strategy, and this calculator includes a simple suggested pre-travel shift.

Does everyone experience jet lag the same way?+

No. Individual factors like sleep habits, age, itinerary, and general health can all influence how noticeable jet lag feels and how long it takes to adjust.

Is this calculator medically accurate?+

This tool provides general educational estimates based on time-zone math and commonly cited travel-adjustment patterns. It is not a medical device, does not diagnose anything, and isn't a substitute for professional medical advice.

Can this calculator guarantee how quickly I will recover?+

No. It provides a general estimate only. Actual adjustment time can be faster or slower depending on individual circumstances, and this tool does not guarantee any particular outcome.
